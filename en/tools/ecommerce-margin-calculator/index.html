What is actually left on each order

How to read the four numbers

Each one answers a different decision. The first changes your price list, the last one changes your advertising plan.

  • Margin per order, after advertisingWhat is left once you have paid goods, the courier, the fees, the share of returns and the cost of acquiring that order. It is the figure you multiply by volume: if it is close to zero, growing takes you nowhere.
  • Highest sustainable acquisition costHow much you could spend on advertising for one order before it stops leaving anything. It is how you read a campaign: an order acquired above this figure loses money regardless of how much it sold.
  • Break-even ROASThe ratio of revenue to ad spend below which advertising consumes the whole margin. It is calculated without fixed costs: it is the line under which raising the budget makes no sense, not the point where the business is profitable.
  • Orders needed to cover fixed costsThe monthly minimum: below it the business pays subscriptions and wages with money it does not produce. If the result is a dash, the margin per order does not even cover itself and fixed costs stay uncovered at any volume.

Why revenue tells you nothing

Growing in sales and getting worse in cash is the most common story that arrives here about six months after a store opens.

Revenue is a sum of full prices. What remains is a subtraction: discounts, coupons, free shipping above a threshold, the payment fee, the return that comes back, the share of advertising that brought that order. None of those appears in the monthly total you look at first, and all of them appear in the account.

The usual mistake is reading gross margin as if it were contribution margin. Gross margin says what the product is worth; contribution margin says what an order is worth, and it is the only one that can carry the acquisition cost and the fixed costs. On an item with a fifty per cent gross margin, an order brought in by a campaign can leave two euro.

The second mistake is reasoning about a single order. Margin per order becomes money only once multiplied by volume, and volume has two constraints: how many orders you can generate and how many you can fulfil without increasing returns. Doubling orders and needing one more person changes the fixed-cost line, not the margin line.

A number you will not find here. No industry average, no "typical" return rate or cost per order. The only pre-filled figures are the field defaults, and they exist to show how the result moves: once you replace them with yours, the example is gone.

Four lines that look small

These four move the result more than the percentage of the payment gateway.

  • Free shipping. It reads as an advantage, it is a cost you absorb. If the threshold that makes it free sits below your margin per order, every free parcel works for the courier.
  • Cash on delivery. It costs the fee, the cash handling and a higher share of undelivered parcels than online payment. It looks like the safest method for the customer and is often the most expensive for you.
  • Free returns. Fine as a commercial choice; put it in the account as a handling cost, which includes the return trip, the inspection and the time of whoever does it.
  • The time of whoever answers. Messages, emails, calls, address changes. It is the line almost nobody puts in fixed costs, and it grows with the orders.

Questions about the tool

Three answers about the calculator, not about your store.

Are the figures I enter sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in the browser, no figure is stored or transmitted. You can close the page without leaving a trace.

Why does the calculator not preload industry averages?

Because an average of that kind would have to be invented. The margin depends on your list, your courier and your own advertising cost: the calculator rearranges those, it does not replace them.

Is a positive margin enough to open an online store?

No. It says whether one order holds up. Whether the channel is worth it depends on the catalogue, the volumes you can realistically reach and who handles orders and returns: that is the conversation we have before writing a scope.

A margin can also be built

Price lists, free-shipping thresholds, bundles, dispatch times: several of those lines can change without touching your prices. If you want to know which ones, start from your numbers.
