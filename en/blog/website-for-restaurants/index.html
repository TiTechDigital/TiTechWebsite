Website for restaurants: features that help win customers

People looking for a place to eat decide in a few seconds, almost always from a phone: they look at the photos, read the menu, check the hours and how to get there. A restaurant website is not there to impress: it is there to make choosing you and booking easy.

What people look for when choosing a restaurant

Three questions, in this order.

First: is it open? Then: what is on the menu and roughly what does it cost? Finally: how do I book and how do I get there? A site that answers these three questions on one screen has already done half the job.

If the answers sit in a PDF you have to pinch to zoom, or in an Instagram story that disappears, many people close the page and try the next restaurant.

The features that bring customers

A few things, done well.

  • Menu as textDishes, prices and, if you have them, allergens and diet options: written in the page, not in an image. It reads on a phone and search engines understand it.
  • Simple bookingPhone and WhatsApp in one tap and a short form (name, date, party size). An online diary makes sense when covers are many.
  • Hours and closuresClosing days, holidays and festive hours updated at once. A wrong opening time costs more than an ugly menu.
  • Real photosDishes, dining room and real people: stock photos are easy to spot.
  • Map and directionsAddress, parking, public transport and a button that opens directions.
  • Groups and eventsA page for group dinners, birthdays and ceremonies, with what you offer and how to ask for a quote.
  • Phone speedLight images and no effects that block the page: most customers arrive from a mobile phone.

Direct bookings and platforms

Platforms bring visibility, the site keeps the contact.

Booking platforms help you get known, but they generally keep a commission and the customer stays tied to them. A booking that arrives from your own site or phone leaves you the contact and costs no commission.

You do not have to choose: use them to be found and bring people to your site as well. Decide who answers requests and how fast, and always confirm: an unanswered request is a lost customer.

Your site and Google profile work together

For "restaurant + city" the map comes before the site.

Correct category, real hours, real photos and reviews from real customers, asked of everyone and without incentives: the profile brings the first call. The site confirms and goes deeper. Name, address and phone must be identical everywhere. Nobody can guarantee a position on Google: you build the right foundations and measure the result over time.

How we set up local presence is explained in SEO consulting; for a local example, read websites for restaurants.

What to avoid

Five things that drive customers away.

  • A PDF-only menu, heavy and unreadable on a phone.
  • Music or video that starts by itself and slows the page.
  • Out-of-date hours, or different from the Google profile.
  • Pop-ups that cover the content as soon as the site opens.
  • Generic text ("genuine traditional cooking") instead of what is actually served.

Frequently asked questions

Does a restaurant need a website if it already has Instagram?

Yes, if it wants to be found by people searching on Google and to give menu, hours and booking in one stable place. Instagram stays the shop window, the site is the base.

How much does a restaurant website cost?

Indicative ranges for the Italian market: a showcase site between 800 and 2,500 euros, a business site between 2,000 and 6,500 euros, an e-commerce between 4,000 and 15,000 euros. The cost depends on the scope. For a quick estimate use the website cost calculator.

Can I update the menu and hours myself?

Yes, it should be that way: the site is built so menu, hours and photos can change without calling anyone.

Do I need an online booking system?

It depends on the number of covers. Many places manage with phone, WhatsApp and a form; a connected diary makes sense when requests are many and arrive out of hours.
