Why no price list exists (and what we publish instead)

A website is not a product with a barcode. The same request ("a new website") can hide five pages and a contact form, or a catalogue, a client area and two integrations with a management system.

Until a year ago, our answer to this question was "we don't publish prices". An honest but convenient position: it left readers to compare only the quotes they had received, when numbers online are hardly scarce. We changed our mind. A business planning a budget has the right to know the orders of magnitude before spending a month on phone calls and forms.

What we publish below therefore has two sides. The numbers, yes: market ranges, a calculator, recurring costs, all VAT excluded, all labelled as estimates. The promise, no: none of these figures is a quote, and a quote born from a table would be as dishonest as a price list. The real scope is built in a conversation, with the list of what the website must do in front of both parties.

There is also a commercial reason, and it is worth saying out loud: the lowest quote almost always wins a comparison of bare numbers. If two agencies write the same label "business website" with very different figures, the lower one looks convenient even when it covers half of the work. Ranges exist precisely for this: to notice when an offer is off scale, downward or upward, before signing it.

Where these figures come from. They synthesise what we see in our own quotes, what competitors ask in real tenders, and the ranges published by comparable agencies in Italy. They are updated for 2026 and, like any market estimate, should be read as an interval, not as a valuation.

Nine factors that move the price

The cost of a website is a sum of decisions. These are the nine that move a quote the most, in typical order of impact. The first five are discussed during the offer stage; the last four are often discovered afterwards.

01

Number of pages and of templates

A twenty-page site reusing three layouts costs less than an eight-page site with eight different structures. Design and development work multiplies by unique templates, not by texts. Extra pages, if identical in the grid, cost content, not code.

02

Custom design

An adapted theme is a reasonable base for many projects. An identity drawn from scratch (type grid, components, micro-interactions) is a different job: it typically weighs 25 to 50% more on the total. Neither is better in the abstract: it depends on how much the site must stand out.

03

CMS and integrations

Deciding who manages content changes the structure. And every external connection (CRM, ERP, payments, newsletter, invoicing) carries analysis, development and testing cost. Integrations are the item that most often escapes anyone comparing only page counts.

04

SEO configuration

The basics (URL structure, headings, sitemap, structured data, performance) are craft today, not an extra, and belong inside the quote of a well-built site. What is paid separately is content work: keyword research, dedicated landing pages, optimisation of large catalogues. Be wary of anyone selling it as a magic subscription.

05

Copywriting

Texts, photos, translations and product pages are the part that most often arrives late. If the agency writes them you pay, typically between 80 and 200 € per page for copy; if you supply them the project gains weeks. Either way somebody has to produce them: the only question is who.

06

E-commerce functionality

Selling is another category of work: catalogue, variants, price lists, payments, shipping, returns, taxation, orders. A shop with two hundred standard product pages and one with per-customer price lists are not the same project, even though both are called "e-commerce".

07

Maintenance

Updates, backups, security, monitoring and support are recurring costs that change the three-year total more than design does. Always ask what is included in the first months, what is paid after, and how much. We cover it at length later in this article.

08

Hosting and domain

Small items that nonetheless reveal the approach: a site with hosting "included at list price forever" can hide the cost of export when you want to leave. Domain, certificates and the production environment must be registered in your name, with credentials you know.

09

Custom development

Client area, quoting tool, bookings, order flows, connectors to the management system: every time the software must do something no existing product does, the work grows by feature, not by page. It is the item that truly separates 5,000 euro from 50,000.

Turn it into a scope

What the calculator does not know. It cannot see the quality of your content, the tidiness of your product database, the decisions you have not made yet. Those are exactly the three things that, in discovery, move the number, usually more than everything you can drag here.

How a quote is built, step by step

A serious quote is not a figure thrown at you. It is the consequence of five steps, and it should be readable and discussable.

Discovery

Start from goals and users, not from pages. How many enquiries you want, from whom, what should happen after the contact. Priorities come out of this.

Scope

List of pages, features, integrations and content, with what is included and what stays out. A written scope is the best defence against surprises.

Estimate

Every item becomes days per role: design, development, content, testing. Asking for the hour breakdown is legitimate, and a serious agency is not offended.

Risks and dependencies

Budget the things that can slow everything: late content, undocumented integrations, postponed decisions. Contingency is not a hidden extra: it is realism.

Offer

Phases, timelines, payment terms, included revisions and acceptance criteria. The quote becomes a plan, not an isolated number.

What a website quote should contain

Seven items. If an offer skips more than one, the total you are comparing is not the same total.

  • Written scopePages and templates listed one by one, features, integrations named after the system they connect to, content included and excluded.
  • Who produces the contentTexts, photos, translations and product pages: names, quantities and usage rights. The most frequently taken-for-granted item, on both sides.
  • Technical quality as a line item, not a promisePerformance, accessibility, on-page SEO, security, backups: they must appear in the document, with the criteria by which they will be verified at the end.
  • Timelines, revisions and dependenciesDated phases, how many revisions per phase, what happens when material arrives late, and who has the final say.
  • Recurring costs kept separateHosting, domain, platform fees, maintenance: a one-off total hides the true cost of the first three years.
  • Ownership and handoverCode, database, domain, accounts, content: in whose name, delivered how, exported in what format. If it is not written down, it is not yours.
  • After launchBug warranty, ordinary support, response times, a human contact. The 90-day plan is worth as much as the discount you got.

How to compare two quotes without being fooled

Six questions to ask both suppliers. The answers matter more than the total.

  • Same scopeAsk for the list of pages, features and integrations. If two quotes do not describe the same work, the comparison is meaningless.
  • What is excludedTexts, photos, translations, hosting, domain, maintenance and legal content are often not included. Better to find out before.
  • Revisions and timingHow many revision rounds are included? What happens when material arrives late? Who holds the final decision?
  • OwnershipCode, domain, accounts, database and content must remain yours. Ask how handover works and what happens if you change supplier.
  • After launchWarranty, support, backups, updates and monitoring: ask for a written plan for the first months.
  • Who actually does the workWho follows the project, who writes, who develops? If selling is one person and the work another, meet the one who will do it before signing.

Two notes from experience: first, the first months after launch should be included in the project quote (it is when bugs surface); second, if hosting is "included free forever" ask what an export costs: the real margin of many agencies is not in the fee, it is in the impossible exit.

The most common mistakes when choosing a web agency

None of these proves bad faith. They are the points where the client most often discovers, with the project already under way, a surprise that could have been a question during the offer stage.

  • Comparing only the totalTwo quotes with different figures can describe different work. Align the scopes before comparing the numbers.
  • Being blinded by the lowest priceThe cheapest is not always clever: often the saving is what was removed from the quote, not a better offer.
  • Not talking to whoever will do the workIf one person sells, another designs and a third develops, meet at least the second before signing.
  • Believing ranking promises"Guaranteed first on Google" is not a service: nobody controls the algorithm. Serious SEO talks about time, method and measurable things.
  • Signing without knowing whose is whatDomain, code, content, photos, accounts: if the contract does not say, you will find out at the end of the relationship.
  • Not asking what happens afterWarranty, support, backups, who answers a failure on a Saturday. The 90-day plan is worth as much as the discount.

Five warning signs inside a quote, for completeness: figures far lower than the others without a scope to justify them; "unlimited revisions" with no definition (unlimited means the project never ends); no written line on timing and dependencies; a proprietary platform with no content export; a single total line with no items. Find two of five in an offer and ask more questions: it is a right before it is advice.

What you can do to contain the cost

The most effective way to spend less is not to ask for a discount: it is to remove uncertainty. A project with clear data and decisions takes fewer hours than one discovering them along the way.

  • Prepare the content first: texts, photos, product data, company details and legal references. They are the number-one cause of delay, and delay costs hours.
  • Cut templates, not necessarily pages: reusing a layout is cheaper than inventing a new one for every page.
  • Start from an MVP: ship what is needed to validate, then add the rest with real data on what was actually missing.
  • Choose standard integrations where possible and ask which ones are truly needed at launch and which can wait six months.
  • Name a single decision-maker: decisions taken by too many people stretch the timeline, and the timeline is the cost.
  • Do not change scope mid-project: every addition has a price and an effect on timing; it belongs in phase two, not inside the current work.

A quote is not a grade. A more intricate project costs more because it requires more work, not because the agency is expensive. The point is understanding whether that work is genuinely needed, and asking yourself that before asking them.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a website cost in Italy in 2026?

The most common ranges we observe on the Italian market, VAT excluded: a one-page or showcase site between 800 and 2,500 €; a business website between 2,000 and 6,500 €; an e-commerce between 4,000 and 15,000 €; a bespoke platform from roughly 12,000 €. These are orders of magnitude, not quotes: the real number comes from the scope.

Are the price ranges in this article quotes?

No. They are market estimates, VAT excluded, useful for understanding the order of magnitude and for spotting when an offer is off scale. No responsible quote can come out of a table: it comes out of a conversation about scope.

Why can two quotes be so different?

Because they are not describing the same work. The number of templates changes, who writes the copy changes, the integrations change, the technical quality changes, and what is included after launch changes. Compare the line items, not just the total.

Template or custom website: which is better?

For many projects a well-adapted theme is the right call: it ships sooner and covers standard needs. Custom design makes sense when the website must do something a template cannot, or when visual identity is part of the value.

How long does a web project take?

A business website typically takes four to eight weeks; an e-commerce six to twelve, if the catalogue and copy are ready. Bespoke platforms depend on flows and integrations. Timelines stretch when content or decisions arrive late.

Does the price include copy and photos?

It depends on the quote. Writing content typically costs between 80 and 200 € per page; professional photography is priced by the day. Ask explicitly whether writing, photos, translations and usage rights are included: they are the items most often taken for granted.

What should a website quote contain?

Seven items at minimum: written scope, who produces the content, technical quality as a line item, timing and revisions, recurring costs kept separate, ownership and handover, and the after-launch plan. If an offer skips more than one, the totals you are comparing are not the same total.

What does it cost to maintain a website each year?

Counting the most common recurring items (hosting, domain, updates, backups, monitoring and support) a well-run business website typically costs between 900 and 3,000 € a year; an e-commerce can exceed 4,000 €. A website abandoned after launch decays: uptime, security and Google rankings included.
