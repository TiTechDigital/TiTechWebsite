How we choose topics

We do not publish to fill a calendar. An article starts when there is a question we actually hear and an answer we can defend.

  • A verifiable intentThe question exists in search and in conversations with clients, not only in a keyword tool.
  • Something to sayWe have direct experience, a method or a mistake to describe. If not, the article does not get written.
  • Checkable numbersWhen we use a number, we explain where it comes from. Market estimates are labelled as estimates.
  • A decision at the endThe reader should be able to make a choice or ask a better question. A text that changes nothing is not useful.

What you will not find here

  • A price list. Cost depends on scope. A number without context is marketing, not a quote.
  • Pay-to-play rankings. We do not rank a supplier first because they paid: when we compare, we also say what we are not convinced by.
  • Promises of first place. Nobody controls the algorithm. We can describe the method and measure what we achieve.

A simple rule: if an article would not help us make a decision, we do not publish it. That applies to us and to the reader.
