Automation and AI for business processes

The hours a company loses are rarely inside the big projects. They are in fifty small steps a day: reading an email, re-typing a figure, hunting for an attachment, updating a sheet. Those steps can be removed.

Where the time is actually saved

Four recurring cases, all with the same characteristic: today someone reads a document and rewrites it somewhere else.

01

Documents become data

Invoices, orders, delivery notes, contracts: the useful fields extracted and entered into the management system, with a review queue for the doubtful cases instead of for all of them.

02

Quotes and replies

Draft quotes built from a written request, with price list and conditions already applied. The person reviews and sends: they don't write it from scratch.

03

First-level support

Answers to repetitive questions (opening hours, availability, order status), with immediate escalation to a human as soon as the request falls outside the script.

04

Synchronisation between systems

One order, one customer or one price entered a single time and propagated wherever it is needed. No export, no "you update the file".

What works and what doesn't

We are explicit about this, because wrong expectations are the first cause of automation projects abandoned after three months.

It works well when the task has a recognisable input, a verifiable output, and an error margin that stays acceptable with a human check in place. Extracting fields from a PDF, classifying a request, proposing a draft: all of that is reliable and measurable today.

It works badly when the decision is delicate, the information is written down nowhere, or the mistake costs more than the time saved. An automation that gets 5% of quotes wrong is worse than no automation at all, if nobody reviews that 5%.

  • Every flow has a human review point until it is proven stable.
  • We measure accuracy and time saved on real cases before widening the scope.
  • No company data is used to train third-party models.

How it starts

One process

We pick the step that costs the most in hours and in errors. One automation at a time, not a digital transformation programme.

Test on real cases

We test it on twenty cases already handled by hand, with the expected result known in advance. It is the fastest way to find out how dirty the source data really is.

Release with control

The flow goes into use with human review and a log of what it did. If something is off, you see it straight away and correct it.

Extension

Only after the saving has been measured do we move to the next process, reusing components already written and tested.

Frequently asked questions

Does AI replace people?

In the work we do, no: it removes the mechanical steps and leaves people in charge of control, exceptions and the relationship with the customer. If a project's stated goal is cutting headcount, it is not the kind of work we take on.

What happens to our company data?

It stays inside the agreed perimeter: suppliers with documented processing terms, no use of your content to train third-party models, retention limited to what is necessary. The details belong in the contract, not only on the website.

What happens when the automation is wrong?

It has to be visible. Every flow records what it processed and with what outcome, and low-confidence cases end up in a review queue. A silent automation is a risk, not a saving.

How much does an automation cost?

What weighs most is how clean the source data is and how many exceptions exist. A half-day analysis on twenty real cases already tells us whether the saving beats the cost. If it doesn't, we say so there, not after three months of work.
