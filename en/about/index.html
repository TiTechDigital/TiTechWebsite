How we work with a client

Our model is direct: the person who joins the first conversation also stays for the delivery. There is no handover between whoever sells and whoever builds, and that shortens the distance between what was promised and what arrives online.

We explain technical choices in plain language. If we propose something different from what you had in mind, we say why and what it means in cost and time. If we think the project is not necessary, we say that before writing a proposal.

Solutions are designed to be maintained: documentation, common standards, no exotic technology only we know how to handle. It is a less exciting choice than others, and it exists so the client is never tied to us in order to keep working.

What we do not do

Saying it first is more useful than saying it later: these are the four requests we decline most often.

  • Cloned pages for every cityThirty identical pages with a different town name in each one do not bring customers. If you serve a territory, we look after the Google profile and the contact page.
  • Guaranteed rankings on GoogleNobody controls the algorithm. Anyone promising first position is selling something they cannot deliver.
  • Bought links and fake reviewsShortcuts you pay for later, when Google notices. We would rather lose a client than propose them.
  • Holding access as leverageDomain, code and content stay the client's. We never keep credentials back as a negotiating position, during the project or after it.
