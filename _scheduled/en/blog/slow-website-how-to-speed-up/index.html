Slow website: how to improve speed and performance

A slow website makes visitors leave before they read a line, especially on a phone, and it can hurt your Google position. The good news is that the causes are few and almost always fixable. Here is how to find them.

In brief

  • The causes are few. Heavy images, too many scripts, weak hosting, fonts and widgets.
  • Measure before acting. PageSpeed Insights and Search Console.
  • Start with images. Often the fix that pays most.
  • The phone is the test bench. That is where slowness is felt most.

The most common causes

Five.

  • Images that are too heavyPhotos of several megabytes where a few hundred kilobytes will do.
  • Too many scripts and componentsPlugins, widgets, trackers and chat all loaded together.
  • Weak hostingA slow server slows even a perfect site: see how to choose hosting.
  • Fonts and effectsHeavy typefaces, animations and opening video.
  • No cachingThe page is rebuilt on every visit.

How to measure

Numbers first.

Test the site on PageSpeed Insights and look in Google Search Console at the page experience report (the so-called Core Web Vitals): they measure how fast the main content appears, how much the page shifts while loading and how well it responds to a tap. Always test on a phone and a normal connection, not just your fast computer.

What to fix first

In order of return.

  • Compress and resize images, and load them as needed.
  • Remove scripts you do not use or load them after the content.
  • Cut fonts and effects.
  • Enable caching and check the hosting.
  • Check again after each change.

Keeping it fast

It is not a one-off job.

A site gets slow again when photos, components and trackers are added without checking. That is why it belongs in website maintenance. If yours is old or overloaded, rebuilding it may be better. To build a fast one see website design.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a website slow?

Mostly heavy images, too many scripts, weak hosting and pointless fonts or effects.

Does speed affect Google?

Partly, yes: a slow site hurts the experience and can weigh on ranking. It is not the only factor.

How fast should a site load?

As fast as possible on a phone: exact times vary, what counts is that the main content appears at once.

Can I speed it up myself?

Images yes, with compression tools. For scripts, caching and hosting you usually need a technician.
