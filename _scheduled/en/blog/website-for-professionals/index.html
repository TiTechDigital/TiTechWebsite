Website for professionals: what should never be missing

People looking for a professional have a problem and little time: they want to understand quickly whether you are the right person and how to take the first step. A practice website sells trust and clarity, not slogans. Here is what should never be missing, whatever the profession.

Areas of practice, written for people outside the trade

People search by problem.

Someone typing "divorce" or "opening a VAT number" into Google is not looking for the word "practice": they are looking for a solution. One page for each area you really work in, with what you do, in which cases and how the first meeting goes, is the base of a good professional website. A few real areas beat a generalist's long list.

The checklist: what must be there

Seven elements.

  • Profile of the professionalName, qualifications, professional body or register, areas and a real photo.
  • Areas of practiceOne page per area, in plain words.
  • How you workFirst meeting, quote, reply times and stages.
  • Complete contact detailsAddress, phone, email, hours and directions.
  • Confidential first contactA short form that asks people not to send case details or documents.
  • Privacy noticeClear and linked to the form.
  • Consistent Google profileSame details as the site, real hours and reviews from real clients.

The rules of the profession

The site adapts to what you may say.

Regulated professions have communication rules: true and verifiable information, no promises of results, no comparisons with other professionals, care with promotional offers. Always check with your professional body what may appear and where: we write the site, but what can be said is decided by whoever answers for the title.

Useful content and trust

A useful explanation is worth more than a slogan.

An article that explains a procedure, a deadline or a right in plain words shows how you work and brings people searching for exactly that answer. It must be written by someone who knows it and kept up to date. For local search, look after the Google profile (category, hours, real reviews) and make the details match the site: see SEO consulting. Nobody can guarantee a position on Google: you build the right foundations and measure the result over time.

Frequently asked questions

Does a professional need a website?

If they want to be found by people who do not know them and to build trust before the first meeting, yes. It can be simple: areas, profile, contact details and a few useful explanations.

How much does a professional practice website cost?

Indicative ranges for the Italian market: a showcase site between 800 and 2,500 euros, a business site between 2,000 and 6,500 euros, an e-commerce between 4,000 and 15,000 euros. The cost depends on the scope. For a quick estimate use the website cost calculator.

Can I publish reviews or client cases?

With care: you need the client's consent, professional secrecy and the rules of the profession. Real reviews on the Google profile are the simplest route.

Can the contact form collect case details?

Better not: a short form (name, contact, area) with a clear notice. Details are shared at the first meeting.
