How to choose hosting for your website

Hosting is where your website lives: speed, availability and security depend on it. Choosing on price alone is the commonest mistake. Here is what to look at, in plain words.

In brief

  • It matters more than design for speed. Slow hosting makes a perfect site slow.
  • Backups and support are not optional. You only notice them when you need them.

The types of hosting

Three routes, in general.

  • SharedSeveral sites on one server: cheap, fine for a simple site with few visits.
  • VPS or cloudReserved resources: for heavier sites or traffic peaks.
  • ManagedThe provider handles updates, security and backups: less work for you.

What really matters

Six criteria.

  • Speed and availability: response times and how often the site is reachable.
  • Backups that are regular and tested, not just declared.
  • Security: HTTPS certificate, updates and protection from attacks.
  • Support you can reach, in your language if you need it.
  • Server location and respect for privacy.
  • Ease of changing provider if one day you must.

Domain and logins: they must be yours

A point people find out late.

The domain (the site's name) and the hosting logins must be in your name, not the agency's or the supplier's. Ask in writing before starting: if you change supplier one day, everything stays yours. It applies to anyone you hire, including us: website maintenance explains what to agree.

What it costs and what not to do

Price alone is not enough.

Hosting is a recurring item: you find it in a site's yearly costs next to domain, updates and backups (in our estimates a well-run business site costs in general between 900 and 3,000 euros a year across all items). Avoid rock-bottom prices with no backups and no support. For the full picture read how much does a website cost in 2026 and website design.

Frequently asked questions

Is shared hosting or VPS better?

For a simple site with few visits shared is enough. For an online store or traffic peaks you need more.

Does hosting affect Google ranking?

Indirectly, through the site's speed and availability. It is not the main factor, but a slow one hurts.

Can I change hosting in the future?

Yes, if the domain and logins are yours and the site is well documented.

Who takes care of backups?

It depends on the contract: decide it and write it down. A backup never tested is only a hope.
